## 6. Major Branches as Responses to Different Problems

### Classical mechanics

**Why it had to emerge:** Motion, force, momentum, and gravity need a transferable framework at ordinary scales.

It isolates trajectories and interactions at ordinary speeds and sizes, providing the limiting language used by engineering and astronomy.

### Electromagnetism and optics

**Why it had to emerge:** Light, charge, and fields cannot be explained as only contact between solid objects.

It treats influence as a condition spread through space, allowing charge, radiation, and light to be related without imagined contact mechanisms.

### Thermodynamics and statistical physics

**Why it had to emerge:** Heat and many-particle systems introduce energy direction, probability, and macroscopic order.

It moves from individual particles to macroscopic variables and asks why energy transformations have an arrow and a limit.

### Quantum physics

**Why it had to emerge:** Atomic-scale matter and radiation force a probabilistic account tied to measurement.

It describes matter and radiation where definite classical paths fail and measurement changes what can be predicted.

### Relativity and cosmology

**Why it had to emerge:** High speed, strong gravity, and the universe require a geometry of spacetime rather than fixed absolute backgrounds.

It makes geometry and gravitation responsive to motion and energy, linking local measurements to the history of the universe.

The branches form a connected map because each isolates a different pressure inside the central question. Their differences are useful only if the viewer can also see where the territories meet.

## 7. Landmark Ideas: Intellectual Motion

### Conservation laws

**BEFORE:** Motion appears to be a sequence of local pushes.

**TENSION:** Complex changes still preserve quantities.

**NEW IDEA:** Invariants constrain what can happen.

**CONSEQUENCE:** Prediction becomes possible beyond one example.

### Fields

**BEFORE:** Objects seem to act only by contact.

**TENSION:** Influence persists across empty space.

**NEW IDEA:** A field describes a structured condition throughout space.

**CONSEQUENCE:** Light, charge, and force share a language.

### Thermodynamics and entropy

**BEFORE:** Energy conservation seems to promise reversibility.

**TENSION:** Real transformations have direction and usable limits.

**NEW IDEA:** Entropy tracks constraints on energy dispersal.

**CONSEQUENCE:** Macroscopic time gains a physical arrow.

### Relativity

**BEFORE:** Space and time appear universal and separate.

**TENSION:** Observers moving differently disagree on measurements.

**NEW IDEA:** Invariants reorganise space and time into spacetime.

**CONSEQUENCE:** Classical mechanics becomes a scale-limited case.

### Quantum theory

**BEFORE:** Small objects seem to possess definite properties independent of observation.

**TENSION:** Atomic experiments yield probabilistic, measurement-linked outcomes.

**NEW IDEA:** The model predicts distributions rather than classical trajectories.

**CONSEQUENCE:** What counts as a physical description changes.

## 8. Historical and Intellectual Shifts

Natural philosophy became mathematical physics through early modern studies of motion and experiment. The nineteenth century added field theory, thermodynamics, and statistical reasoning. Twentieth-century relativity and quantum theory showed that classical laws are powerful limits rather than the final picture. Contemporary physics joins precision instruments, computation, laboratories, and cosmological observation to test where present models meet their boundaries.

Read this history as changes in what could be asked, measured, compared, or made accountable—not as a parade of names. The field's present map is layered over older questions and remains open to revision.

## 11. Limits, Disagreements, and Frontiers

Models gain power by simplifying. They can fail at interacting scales, uncertain measurements, complex many-body behaviour, or unresolved theoretical boundaries. A physics claim is mature when its domain of validity, idealisations, and possible falsifying observations are visible.
