## 1. Field Identity and Boundaries

Statistics is the disciplined design and analysis of evidence under variation: it makes uncertainty, sampling, comparison, prediction, and decision visible rather than pretending noise is a nuisance that can be ignored. The field is distinct from its neighbours because it selects a characteristic object, scale, or standard of explanation, while real problems often require translations across boundaries.

## 2. Central Question

How can we infer reliable patterns about a population or process from noisy, incomplete, and potentially biased observations?

## 3. Why This Class of Problems Matters

### Field Becomes Necessary When

Variation becomes too large for anecdote and intuition, decisions concern groups rather than only observed cases, and evidence must carry uncertainty instead of hiding it.

**Opening problem (editorial thought experiment, not a historical event):** A small sample contains real signals mixed with measurement error, selection, missing cases, and chance. People must decide whether a pattern will travel beyond the observations that first revealed it.

## 4. Objects of Inquiry

populations and samples, variables and measurements, distributions, experiments, observational studies, models, predictions, and decisions under uncertainty

## 5. How This Field Thinks

Statistics is the disciplined design and analysis of evidence under variation: it makes uncertainty, sampling, comparison, prediction, and decision visible rather than pretending noise is a nuisance that can be ignored. Its method is not a personality trait. It is a disciplined sequence of questions: what is being claimed, which comparison or interpretation supports it, what assumptions make the inference possible, and what evidence could narrow or overturn it.

**Methods / evidence:**
- measurement design
- random and stratified sampling
- experiments and quasi-experiments
- regression and probabilistic models
- sensitivity, calibration, and robustness checks

## 6. Major Branches as Responses to Different Problems

### Descriptive statistics

**Why it had to emerge:** Distributions, summaries, and visualisations make variation visible before explanation.

It describes distributions and variation before asking what caused them, preventing a summary from masquerading as an explanation.

### Probability and statistical inference

**Why it had to emerge:** Uncertainty about samples and populations needs a formal language.

It turns repeated sampling into a calibrated language for estimates, uncertainty, and what procedures would do over the long run.

### Regression and causal inference

**Why it had to emerge:** Relationships, confounding, and interventions require explicit comparisons and assumptions.

It separates association from intervention by making counterfactual comparisons and confounding assumptions explicit.

### Survey and sampling methodology

**Why it had to emerge:** Who is observed, who is missing, and how units are selected determine what can generalise.

It studies how a sample enters a dataset, who is missing, and which selection mechanism limits generalisation.

### Bayesian, computational, and decision statistics

**Why it had to emerge:** Complex models and decisions update beliefs while keeping loss, prior information, and computation visible.

It uses computation, prior information, loss, and robustness checks when decisions need models more complex than a hand calculation.

The branches form a connected map because each isolates a different pressure inside the central question. Their differences are useful only if the viewer can also see where the territories meet.

## 7. Landmark Ideas: Intellectual Motion

### Probability and sampling

**BEFORE:** A count seems to speak for the whole.

**TENSION:** Observed cases are partial and variable.

**NEW IDEA:** Probability describes what samples can and cannot tell us.

**CONSEQUENCE:** Uncertainty becomes explicit rather than hidden.

### Law of large numbers and the central limit

**BEFORE:** More observations look like mere repetition.

**TENSION:** Aggregates stabilise in ways individual cases do not.

**NEW IDEA:** Sampling distributions make estimation possible.

**CONSEQUENCE:** Precision becomes a property of design and variation.

### Randomisation

**BEFORE:** A before/after difference appears causal.

**TENSION:** Groups differ in hidden ways.

**NEW IDEA:** Random assignment balances expected confounders.

**CONSEQUENCE:** Intervention gains a defensible comparison.

### Regression and causal inference

**BEFORE:** Correlation appears to explain a relationship.

**TENSION:** Confounding and selection can mimic effects.

**NEW IDEA:** Models make assumptions and counterfactual comparisons visible.

**CONSEQUENCE:** Prediction and causation are separated.

### Reproducibility and robustness

**BEFORE:** A significant result looks final.

**TENSION:** Analysis choices and selective reporting alter conclusions.

**NEW IDEA:** Sensitivity, replication, and open data test stability.

**CONSEQUENCE:** Evidence includes how a result survives scrutiny.

## 8. Historical and Intellectual Shifts

Statistics grew from censuses, state records, insurance, astronomy, and error analysis. Least-squares methods and probability made noisy observations tractable; twentieth-century experimental design and inference formalised comparison and decision; computers expanded simulation, data collection, and model complexity. Current practice increasingly confronts missingness, selection, causal assumptions, reproducibility, and algorithmic bias.

Read this history as changes in what could be asked, measured, compared, or made accountable—not as a parade of names. The field's present map is layered over older questions and remains open to revision.

**Difference that must remain visible:** Statistics foregrounds sampling, uncertainty, and inference; computing foregrounds representation, algorithms, and systems.

## 11. Limits, Disagreements, and Frontiers

No procedure removes the assumptions built into measurement, sampling, model choice, or decision loss. Statistical significance can coexist with trivial effect, and prediction can succeed without causal understanding. A responsible analysis reports uncertainty, missingness, sensitivity, and the population to which the claim applies.

## 12. General Learner Mental Model

Ask what was measured, who or what was observed, what comparison was made, what uncertainty remains, and which decision the analysis is meant to support.
