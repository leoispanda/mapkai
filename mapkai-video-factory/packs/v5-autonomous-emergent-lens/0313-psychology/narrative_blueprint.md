## Field

Psychology (0313)

## Central Question

Why can the same person sincerely think, feel, and act differently when attention, memory, biology, relationships, and situation change?

## Field Becomes Necessary When

Introspection and moral judgement cannot reliably explain patterned behaviour, yet decisions about learning, care, work, and relationships depend on mechanisms that people cannot fully observe from inside themselves.

## Narrative Engine and Opening Hook

**Selected engine:** intellectual conflict.

Several witnesses remember the same event with complete sincerity. Then one small change in the question alters a detail they were certain they saw. Is the mind a recorder—or an active constructor?

The opening must establish a real intellectual problem before any field definition or branch map. Do not start with “Psychology is the study of…” or “there are several branches…”.

## Parallel Civilization Entry

**What they already know:**
- first-person experience
- stories about character
- customs for teaching and care
- observation of habits
- informal knowledge of emotion and relationships

**What they do not yet know:** the formal concept, method, institution, or field name that our civilization uses for the pattern.

**Trigger event:** Sincere reports, repeated behaviour patterns, and failed predictions show that subjective certainty is not a complete window into mental mechanisms.

**Opening problem:** A person can explain their own action convincingly after the fact, but the same behaviour changes with attention, reward, sleep, social pressure, bodily state, and the way a choice is framed.

## Ordinary Intuition and Its Failure

**Ordinary intuition:** People know why they acted and memories replay what happened; behaviour is mainly a stable expression of character.

**Why it fails:** Memory is reconstructed, attention is selective, behaviour is situational, and biological and social processes can influence a person without becoming conscious explanations.

## Central Tension

How can a mind scientifically study a mind that does not fully understand itself?

## First Intellectual Turn

The narrator lets the viewer recognise the pattern before naming it: a local solution or confident explanation works once, then breaks when the scale, comparison, observer, institution, or consequence changes. The turn must be causal, not an ornamental surprise.

## Primary Aha

The reversal is not that people are irrational or dishonest. It is that an adaptive, predictive mind can be sincere while its confidence is produced by mechanisms that still need independent evidence.

## Secondary Turns

1. A useful mental shortcut can produce a systematic bias when the environment changes.
2. Learning is not only storage; consequences and predictions reshape future behaviour.
3. Development changes what a person can perceive, regulate, and remember across a life.
4. A situation can make a disposition visible, mute it, or create behaviour no trait label predicted.

## Concept, Method, and Discipline Emergence

- **1.** people notice experience and behaviour
- **2.** stories and character explanations meet recurring anomalies
- **3.** measurement separates report, behaviour, and mechanism
- **4.** learning and cognition become experimental objects
- **5.** social and biological levels are integrated
- **6.** clinical practice links explanation to care and accountability

The sequence is an editorial logic. Let each new tool answer a pressure created by the previous limitation; do not read the arrows aloud as a formula.

## Return to Our Civilisation

The witnesses were an editorial thought experiment. Our psychology is the real, imperfect, evidence-seeking field that studies minds as biological, developmental, social, and clinical processes.

State plainly that the prototype was fictional and that the modern field is real.

## Modern Field Expansion

Psychology studies minds in bodies and situations by treating experience as meaningful evidence while testing confidence, mechanism, development, and context with methods stronger than introspection alone.

### Branch Integration

- **Cognitive psychology:** Attention, perception, memory, language, and reasoning need models of information processing.
- **Developmental psychology:** Change across the lifespan cannot be explained by a static adult mind.
- **Social psychology:** Influence, identity, groups, and situations alter behaviour beyond individual traits.
- **Biological and neuropsychology:** Brains, bodies, emotion, and behaviour constrain and enable mental processes.
- **Clinical and counselling psychology:** Distress, assessment, intervention, and recovery test how explanation becomes care.

### Landmark Integration

The video must explain why each landmark changed the field's mental model. Preserve the BEFORE → TENSION → NEW IDEA → CONSEQUENCE motion from the factual source; do not turn the landmarks into name-dropping.

### Cross-Field Connections

### Education science (0111)

**Connection:** Learning, development, attention, and motivation shape how knowledge is taught.

**Difference that must remain visible:** Psychology studies mechanisms; education designs social practices and institutions around learning.

### Medicine and health (09)

**Connection:** Mental and bodily health influence each other and require integrated care.

**Difference that must remain visible:** Medicine diagnoses and treats bodily conditions; psychology explains mental and behavioural mechanisms and interventions.

### Sociology and cultural studies (0314)

**Connection:** Individuals develop inside norms, institutions, identities, and unequal structures.

**Difference that must remain visible:** Psychology focuses on person-level mechanisms; sociology traces patterned relations and institutions.

### Statistics (0542)

**Connection:** Measurement reliability, uncertainty, and replication determine what psychological evidence can support.

**Difference that must remain visible:** Statistics provides inference tools; psychology supplies theories and measures of mind and behaviour.
