## 1. Field Identity and Boundaries

Psychology studies minds in bodies and situations by treating experience as meaningful evidence while testing confidence, mechanism, development, and context with methods stronger than introspection alone. The field is distinct from its neighbours because it selects a characteristic object, scale, or standard of explanation, while real problems often require translations across boundaries.

## 2. Central Question

Why can the same person sincerely think, feel, and act differently when attention, memory, biology, relationships, and situation change?

## 3. Why This Class of Problems Matters

### Field Becomes Necessary When

Introspection and moral judgement cannot reliably explain patterned behaviour, yet decisions about learning, care, work, and relationships depend on mechanisms that people cannot fully observe from inside themselves.

**Opening problem (editorial thought experiment, not a historical event):** A person can explain their own action convincingly after the fact, but the same behaviour changes with attention, reward, sleep, social pressure, bodily state, and the way a choice is framed.

## 4. Objects of Inquiry

perception, attention, memory, learning, emotion, development, social influence, brain–body processes, and distress or recovery

## 5. How This Field Thinks

Psychology studies minds in bodies and situations by treating experience as meaningful evidence while testing confidence, mechanism, development, and context with methods stronger than introspection alone. Its method is not a personality trait. It is a disciplined sequence of questions: what is being claimed, which comparison or interpretation supports it, what assumptions make the inference possible, and what evidence could narrow or overturn it.

**Methods / evidence:**
- controlled experiments
- behavioural measurement
- longitudinal and developmental studies
- clinical evidence and case formulation
- statistical modelling and replication

## 6. Major Branches as Responses to Different Problems

### Cognitive psychology

**Why it had to emerge:** Attention, perception, memory, language, and reasoning need models of information processing.

It builds models of information processing from attention and perception through memory, language, and reasoning.

### Developmental psychology

**Why it had to emerge:** Change across the lifespan cannot be explained by a static adult mind.

It treats change itself as an object, following how capacities, relationships, and regulation develop across a lifespan.

### Social psychology

**Why it had to emerge:** Influence, identity, groups, and situations alter behaviour beyond individual traits.

It tests how norms, roles, identity, and group presence alter behaviour that a trait label alone cannot predict.

### Biological and neuropsychology

**Why it had to emerge:** Brains, bodies, emotion, and behaviour constrain and enable mental processes.

It connects mental processes to brain, body, emotion, sleep, and biological constraints without reducing experience to a single organ.

### Clinical and counselling psychology

**Why it had to emerge:** Distress, assessment, intervention, and recovery test how explanation becomes care.

It turns explanation toward assessment, intervention, distress, and recovery while keeping evidence and ethics visible.

The branches form a connected map because each isolates a different pressure inside the central question. Their differences are useful only if the viewer can also see where the territories meet.

## 7. Landmark Ideas: Intellectual Motion

### Conditioning and learning

**BEFORE:** Behaviour seems to express fixed character.

**TENSION:** Consequences and predictions alter future behaviour.

**NEW IDEA:** Learning mechanisms make change experimentally tractable.

**CONSEQUENCE:** Environment becomes part of explanation.

### Cognitive schemas and biases

**BEFORE:** Reasoning appears transparent to itself.

**TENSION:** Shortcuts systematically misfire in unfamiliar conditions.

**NEW IDEA:** Mental representations can be measured as mechanisms.

**CONSEQUENCE:** Error becomes patterned rather than merely personal.

### Memory reconstruction

**BEFORE:** Remembering seems like replay.

**TENSION:** Questions and later information alter confident reports.

**NEW IDEA:** Memory is reconstructed from traces, expectations, and context.

**CONSEQUENCE:** Sincerity separates from accuracy without dismissing experience.

### Development and attachment

**BEFORE:** The adult mind appears fully formed.

**TENSION:** Capacity and regulation change through relationships and time.

**NEW IDEA:** Development becomes a process with biological and social conditions.

**CONSEQUENCE:** A life course replaces a static model.

### Person–situation interaction

**BEFORE:** Traits seem to predict behaviour everywhere.

**TENSION:** Roles, norms, and settings alter what people do.

**NEW IDEA:** Disposition and situation must be modelled together.

**CONSEQUENCE:** Individual and social explanations become complementary.

## 8. Historical and Intellectual Shifts

Psychology separated from philosophy through nineteenth-century laboratories and measurement. Behaviourism made observable learning central; psychoanalytic and humanistic traditions foregrounded meaning and distress; the cognitive revolution returned attention to internal processes; neuroscience linked mind to brain and body. Replication and open-science reforms now ask which effects are robust, contextual, and practically useful.

Read this history as changes in what could be asked, measured, compared, or made accountable—not as a parade of names. The field's present map is layered over older questions and remains open to revision.

## 10. Cross-Field Connections

### Education science (0111)

**Connection:** Learning, development, attention, and motivation shape how knowledge is taught.

**Difference that must remain visible:** Psychology studies mechanisms; education designs social practices and institutions around learning.

### Medicine and health (09)

**Connection:** Mental and bodily health influence each other and require integrated care.

**Difference that must remain visible:** Medicine diagnoses and treats bodily conditions; psychology explains mental and behavioural mechanisms and interventions.

### Sociology and cultural studies (0314)

**Connection:** Individuals develop inside norms, institutions, identities, and unequal structures.

**Difference that must remain visible:** Psychology focuses on person-level mechanisms; sociology traces patterned relations and institutions.

### Statistics (0542)

**Connection:** Measurement reliability, uncertainty, and replication determine what psychological evidence can support.

**Difference that must remain visible:** Statistics provides inference tools; psychology supplies theories and measures of mind and behaviour.

## 11. Limits, Disagreements, and Frontiers

Mental mechanisms are inferred, not directly watched. Measures can be culturally narrow, effects can be context-sensitive, and clinical categories carry ethical and institutional consequences. Confidence, vividness, and statistical significance are not identical to truth or usefulness.

## 12. General Learner Mental Model

Treat experience as important evidence but never the whole explanation; ask what mechanism, comparison, context, and independent measure could support a psychological claim.
