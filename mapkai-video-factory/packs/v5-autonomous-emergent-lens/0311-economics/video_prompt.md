## Master creative direction

Create an intellectually serious journey through Economics. This is not a generic educational explainer, Wikipedia summary, or line-by-line narration script. Use the factual 'overview.md' as the authority for what is true and the separate 'narrative_blueprint.md' as the route for why the knowledge became necessary. Preserve NotebookLM/Gemini Notebook freedom to select, compress, narrate, visualise, and pace.

## Opening and central question

Open with this problem before naming the field:

At dawn, every household opens its chest and finds twice as many tokens as the night before. No new bread has been baked. No extra house has appeared. No doctor has gained another hour. No field has become larger. Yet everyone can now offer twice as much. By evening, has the settlement become richer, or have its people merely changed the numbers attached to scarcity?

Central question: **How can a society coordinate countless competing uses of scarce resources when every choice closes off other possible futures, and when the consequences of those choices spread through prices, institutions, power, and distribution?**

Selected narrative engine: **A paradox that becomes a system cascade: a society can possess more money without possessing more wealth, because claims on resources and the resources themselves are different things; once this distinction is seen, prices, production, exchange, inflation, institutions, inequality, and policy emerge as connected problems of coordination.**. Generate curiosity through a mystery, paradox, dilemma, discovery, conflict, journey, or scale change that is causally necessary to the explanation.

## What not to reveal too early

Do not begin with “Economics is the study of…”, “there are several branches…”, a glossary, or a broad claim that could fit any field. Do not say the modern term before the fictional observer has experienced, noticed, compared, and generalised the pattern. Do not use childish characters, classroom dialogue, cheesy motivation, corporate language, or a made-up historical authority.

## Intellectual turns

Primary Aha: The deepest economic problem is not that resources are scarce. People have always known scarcity. The reversal is that scarcity becomes difficult when choices interact: what one person can afford, produce, earn, save, or consume depends on what others do in response. Economics begins when we stop asking only which choice is best and start asking how choices become a system.

Secondary turns to weave naturally:
- A price is not merely a number attached to an object; it is a compressed signal emerging from competing claims, constraints, expectations, institutions, and bargaining power.
- Exchange does not eliminate scarcity. It reorganises who uses scarce resources, for what purpose, and under which incentives.
- What is sensible individually can become unstable collectively: saving can reduce spending, wage cuts can reduce demand, panic can create the shortage people fear, and attempts to buy protection can move prices for everyone.
- Efficiency and distribution are not the same question. A system may allocate resources productively while still producing outcomes that societies judge unequal, insecure, or politically unacceptable.
- Markets are not the absence of institutions. Property rights, contracts, money, firms, regulation, taxation, norms, and enforcement are among the structures that make particular forms of market coordination possible.

Every turn must alter the viewer's model of the problem. A branch or landmark is earned when the previous explanation creates a new pressure; never recite a numbered list.

## Mandatory knowledge coverage

The finished video must naturally cover: field identity and boundaries; central question; why this class of problems matters; how the field thinks and what evidence it uses; major branches and why each exists; 3–6 landmark ideas with BEFORE → TENSION → NEW IDEA → CONSEQUENCE; historical/intellectual shifts; real-world applications; cross-field connections; limits/frontiers; and a final learner mental model. The viewer must be able to answer what the field is fundamentally trying to understand.

## Return to Earth / MapKAI

The parallel prototype is fictional. Return explicitly to our civilisation: Return to the settlement after the token windfall. Its people are no longer asking whether twice as many tokens made them twice as rich. They now ask a more powerful set of questions: what real resources exist, what prevents their expansion, how competing claims are coordinated, who gains access to them, what signals guide production, and which rules shape the outcome. The mystery of the doubled tokens has become a window into the architecture of an economy.

For the General Overview, end at the existing MapKAI knowledge map—not at a new fictional taxonomy. For a conventional field, end with the real field's current connected map and its neighbouring fields.

## Visual direction

Use visual reasoning rather than decorative stock montage:
- Animate the opening settlement as token balances double while the physical inventory of homes, bread, labour hours, tools, and medical capacity remains visually fixed; then let bids and prices begin to rise.
- Show one scarce plot of land branching into alternative futures—food, housing, factory, conservation—to make opportunity cost visible as sacrificed possibilities rather than a monetary expense.
- Visualise a price as the meeting point of many moving forces: households entering and leaving, production expanding, shortages appearing, substitutes becoming available, expectations shifting, and regulations changing.
- Create a cascading network in which a single change in interest rates travels through borrowing, investment, employment, income, spending, housing, exchange rates, and expectations.
- Contrast an individual action with its aggregate counterpart: one household saving more versus every household saving more at once.
- Use two layers over the same economy: a physical layer of people, machines, land, goods, and time, and a nominal layer of money, prices, wages, debt, and financial claims.
- Show identical market exchanges under different institutional rules—different property rights, bargaining power, taxes, or external costs—to demonstrate that markets always operate inside a rule system.
- End with a zoom from one ordinary purchase outward into supply chains, wages, financing, energy, regulation, trade, environmental effects, and income distribution.

Let diagrams show constraints, scales, evidence, feedback, boundaries, or transformations. Use a restrained documentary palette and adult pacing.

## Ending transformation

End with a changed mental model, not a product pitch:
**See an economy as a continuously adapting coordination system built on real scarcity. People and organisations choose under constraints; their choices interact; prices and institutions transmit information and incentives; production changes the feasible set; money and finance organise claims across time; distribution determines who can command resources; and feedback can produce outcomes no participant intended. Economics is the toolkit for tracing that chain from trade-off to choice, from choice to interaction, and from interaction to collective consequence.**

## Anti-generic guardrail

Reject filler, mechanical enumeration, repeated “important because” sentences, sentimental uplift, and any scene that could be moved to another field by replacing three nouns. The story should help the learner understand why this field became necessary; the factual source should make the modern knowledge map trustworthy.
