# MapKAI Intellectual Journey: Physics

Create a MapKAI Video Overview for **Physics** (物理学), within the wider knowledge continent of **Natural Sciences, Mathematics and Statistics**.

This is not a generic educational explainer and not a Wikipedia summary. The intended form is:

**Documentary × Intellectual Fable × Knowledge Map**

Address an intelligent adult learner in precise, vivid English. The video should make the field feel discovered rather than recited.

## Narrative device

Build the opening and structure around this intellectual device:

> A beam of light races beside a moving train: if both observers measure the same speed of light, what must give way—distance, time, or common sense?

Treat it as a thought experiment, dilemma, mystery, journey, discovery, or paradox—not as a childish fictional story. The device must do explanatory work: it should create a genuine “Wait—why?” moment, then make the field's way of thinking feel necessary.

## Required intellectual journey

Let the narrative move naturally through these ideas, rather than announcing a numbered syllabus:

1. Establish the **central question** and the puzzle it creates.
2. Show **why the field emerged**: what older intuition, practice, or explanation was no longer enough.
3. Reveal **how the field thinks**—its characteristic evidence, methods, models, or interpretive moves.
4. Bring in the **major branches** as different responses to one shared question, not a mechanical list.
5. Weave in **3–6 landmark ideas** as turning points that change what the learner can see.
6. Draw at least two meaningful **connections to other MapKAI fields**.
7. Close with a final mental model / intellectual insight: a compact way to see how the field fits together.

## Knowledge Spine to use

Central question: How can a few deep principles explain both a falling apple and the behaviour of stars, atoms, light, and time?

How it thinks: Physics begins by isolating a phenomenon, measuring it carefully, expressing relationships mathematically, and testing whether a model predicts new observations. A good law is not a slogan: it survives contact with experiments and reveals where it stops applying.

Landmark ideas:
- Conservation laws: quantities such as energy and momentum constrain what can happen.
- Fields: objects can influence one another through structured conditions spread across space.
- Thermodynamics: energy transformations have direction and limits.
- Relativity: measurements of space and time depend on motion, while the speed of light remains invariant.
- Quantum theory: prediction at small scales is probabilistic and depends on how systems are measured.

Connections:
- Mathematics (0541) supplies the language of models and proof.
- Chemistry (0531) applies physical laws to matter and reactions.
- Earth sciences (0532) and environmental sciences (0521) use physical models of complex systems.
- Electronics and automation (0714) turns physical principles into technology.

General-learner outcome: A general learner should see physics as a disciplined search for invariants: identify what changes, what is conserved, what can be measured, and which model is adequate at this scale.

## Guardrails

- Story serves understanding; it is never decorative filler.
- Do not use childish fictional characters, a children's-story tone, classroom dialogue, cheesy motivation, or a list-style lecture.
- Do not say “there are five branches” and mechanically enumerate them.
- Do not invent statistics, historical scenes, quotations, or claims of a single founder.
- Do not treat an optional MapKAI fable as a documentary source.
- End after the final intellectual conclusion. Do not add branding, calls to action, or an outro; MapKAI adds its branded outro later in post-production.

The learner should finish thinking both: “That was genuinely interesting” and “I now understand how this field fits together.”
