1. Establish the **central question** and the puzzle it creates.
2. Show **why the field emerged**: what older intuition, practice, or explanation was no longer enough.
3. Reveal **how the field thinks**—its characteristic evidence, methods, models, or interpretive moves.
4. Bring in the **major branches** as different responses to one shared question, not a mechanical list.
5. Weave in **3–6 landmark ideas** as turning points that change what the learner can see.
6. Draw at least two meaningful **connections to other MapKAI fields**.

## Guardrails

- Story serves understanding; it is never decorative filler.
- Do not use childish fictional characters, a children's-story tone, classroom dialogue, cheesy motivation, or a list-style lecture.
- Do not say “there are five branches” and mechanically enumerate them.
- Do not invent statistics, historical scenes, quotations, or claims of a single founder.
- Do not treat an optional MapKAI fable as a documentary source.
- End after the final intellectual conclusion. Do not add branding, calls to action, or an outro; MapKAI adds its branded outro later in post-production.

The learner should finish thinking both: “That was genuinely interesting” and “I now understand how this field fits together.”
