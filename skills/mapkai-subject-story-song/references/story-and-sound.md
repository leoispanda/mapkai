# Adaptation and sound selection

## The narrative spine

Write down what the character wants, what the old approach gets right, the specific friction it cannot handle, the character's changed action and what that action makes possible. Keep a limit or remaining question where the knowledge requires one.

A song needs that causal sequence even when it compresses a longer story. Useful movement is:

- First verse: enter the task through an object and an initially reasonable approach.
- First refrain: name the character's need or question in natural, memorable words.
- Next verse: show the obstacle and the new observation, interpretation, test or coordination.
- Bridge or last verse: perform the decisive change; do not substitute a declaration of hope.
- Last refrain/ending: return to the same object or question with changed meaning.

This is a useful pattern, not a compulsory stanza count. Narrative rap can use a continuous verse; a quiet scene can use fewer, longer phrases. Match the story's sentences to a singable breath and natural English stress. Let an instrumental passage carry a pause, never an essential unsung explanation.

The beat-to-lyric mapping should identify where the task, friction, field method, turn and ending appear. The lyrics must make sense without reading the separate explanation. Different subjects can share a sound while their actions, imagery and hook remain distinct.

## Preferred sounds

### Ethereal bell-pop

The owner describes a light "叮咚" rhythm and an ethereal feeling. Use that sensory target: soft bell/glockenspiel or glassy plucked notes, felt piano, restrained acoustic or small string colour, airy space, a gently moving pulse and an intelligible adult lead. Dream-pop or delicate chamber-pop may be useful labels; neither a label nor ordinary guitar folk alone establishes the desired feel.

Fits close observation, quiet discovery, making and interpretation, memory, or a small change of understanding. The arrangement can have movement and curiosity; "ethereal" does not mean motionless, washed-out vocals or a childish music-box tune. Begin from roughly 78–100 BPM when suitable, and adjust to the story.

The previously approved Leave a Little Room direction used 84 BPM, fingerpicked acoustic guitar, felt piano, acoustic bass, brushed percussion and small cello/viola details. This is an owned warmth/space reference, not a requirement to copy its melody, lyrics, instrument list or genre classification. The latest bell-like description controls the new preference.

### Memphis hip-hop

The owner's established reference is the bright Memphis-inspired MapKAI/city-song direction: about 96 BPM, deep rounded 808s, dry snare/claps, triplet hi-hats, acoustic plucks and sparse cowbell, with clear melodic rap and a sung hook. Use roughly 88–104 BPM where the words fit. It can stay sunny, warm and conversational.

Fits accounts that conflict, negotiation, investigations, successive choices, case patterns or a fast sequence of practical handoffs. Put narrative detail in rhythmically clear verses and give the refrain a memorable melody. The owner dislikes heavy metal, screaming and harsh distortion; do not interpret "Memphis" as an automatic request for aggressive dark phonk or distorted cowbell dominance.

## Other styles

Their equal shares are defined in `genre-policy.json`.

| Family | Narrative fit | Musical emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acoustic folk-pop | Grounded daily work, a journey, seasonal observation | Guitar-led intimacy; distinct from the bell-led ethereal preference |
| Soul-pop | Dialogue, care, relationships, asking and responding | Rhodes/piano, round bass, expressive but restrained vocal |
| Electronic art-pop | Observation, tests, mechanisms and an evolving discovery | Pulses/layers develop with the story, intelligible lead |
| Indie-pop | Iteration, building, repair, an ordinary decision changing a task | Light rhythmic motif, clean guitar/piano and a clear refrain |
| Funk-pop | Movement, coordination, service and team handoffs | Bass groove and syncopation reflect the sequence of actions |

These are candidate fits, not fixed subject assignments. Health can tell a rhythmic coordination story, science can tell a quiet observation story, and humanities can contain a lively dispute. Select by the actual story, then use the series balance among suitable options.

## Production distinctions

- Design a new motif and imagery for each song. Familiar texture does not require copying an earlier hook.
- Separate the style prompt from the full ordered lyrics. Arrangement instructions and silent labels are not extra sung text.
- Give every essential action enough vocal time. A rap verse can carry more words than a sustained sung passage, but density must stay intelligible.
- A story about a historical person's work does not license invented direct quotations or claims about their private feelings.
- Document requested style separately from what the actual performance supports. A prompt or an ASR transcript alone does not certify musical genre or vocal quality.
